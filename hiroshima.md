# Hiroshima

**Days:** 1 (together with friend, part of the reconvened group leg)

## Overview

Given only 1 day and wanting to take real time with the Peace Memorial, Miyajima
likely doesn't fit — the museum deserves its full time, and Miyajima needs roughly
another half day (ferry + real time on the island) on top of that. The day should be
built around the Peace Memorial as the anchor, with Miyajima as a bonus only if hours
remain, not something to plan around fitting in.

## Recommendations by theme

### Historic / memorial (the anchor of the day)
- **Peace Memorial Museum** — walks through personal accounts and artifacts from the
  atomic bombing; most visitors spend 2-3 hours. Wants real time here, not rushed.
- **Peace Memorial Park** — includes the **A-Bomb Dome** (preserved skeletal ruin),
  the **Children's Peace Monument** (covered in paper cranes, tied to Sadako Sasaki's
  story), and the **Memorial Cenotaph**.
- This is a heavy, emotional site — don't schedule anything demanding immediately
  after; leave quiet buffer time.

### If time allows
- **Miyajima** — famous "floating" torii gate (appears to float at high tide),
  friendly roaming deer (similar to Nara), known for oysters. Ferry departs from
  Miyajimaguchi, reachable by tram/train from central Hiroshima. Ferry alone is
  ~1 hour round trip; realistically needs another 2-3 hours for the island itself
  to not feel rushed.

### Nature (group loves this category)
- **Mount Misen** (on Miyajima) — a forested mountain reachable by ropeway, with
  hiking trails and wild monkeys near the summit alongside sweeping views over the
  Seto Inland Sea. This is really the nature payoff of a Miyajima trip, beyond the
  torii gate itself — worth knowing about even though Miyajima overall is only
  "if time allows" here.
- **Shukkeien Garden** — a traditional landscape garden in central Hiroshima
  itself, much lower time commitment than Miyajima; a real option if the day
  doesn't stretch to the ferry trip but some nature/garden time is still wanted.
