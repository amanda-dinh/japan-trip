# Nagoya / Ghibli Park

**Days:** 1 full day for Ghibli Park (together with friend, her must-see too)

## Overview

Ghibli Park is a must-do for both — full day dedicated to it, no split with Nagoya
city time. No particular favorite film/area, wants to see everything, so this means
targeting the Premium All-Access Pass over individual area tickets.

## Ghibli Park — the five areas

- **Ghibli's Grand Warehouse** — largest indoor area, exhibition zones and film set
  recreations across multiple movies, plus a cinema showing original Ghibli shorts.
  Budget ~3 hours alone.
- **Hill of Youth** — antique shop from *Whisper of the Heart*, Cat Bureau from
  *The Cat Returns*.
- **Dondoko Forest** — includes Satsuki & Mei's House from *My Neighbor Totoro*
  (interior access requires Premium ticket, not Standard).
- **Mononoke Village**
- **Valley of Witches** — newest area, Howl's Moving Castle, Okino Residence, Witch's
  House (interior access also requires Premium).

A full visit to all five areas takes ~6-8 hours.

## Ticket logistics (time-sensitive — needs attention early)

- Tickets release at **2pm JST on the 10th of the month, two months before the visit
  date**. For a February 2026 visit, that's a December on-sale date.
- Since the goal is seeing everything, get the **Premium All-Access Pass**, not
  individual area tickets — Standard doesn't include interior access to Satsuki &
  Mei's House or the Howl's Castle buildings, and there's no day-of upgrade.
- International visitors use the official overseas purchase page, which has fewer
  time slots than the domestic version — book within the first 15-30 minutes of
  sales opening, as traffic is heaviest then.
- Passport number required at booking; passport shown at the gate.

## Access

- Ghibli Park is in Nagakute, Aichi, inside Expo 2005 Aichi Commemorative Park —
  about 45 minutes from central Nagoya.
- Given the 6-8 hour visit, plan an early start from the Nagoya hotel.

### Nature (group loves this category)
- The park itself sits inside the larger **Expo 2005 Aichi Commemorative Park
  (Moricoro Park)**, which has forested walking paths, gardens, and a satoyama
  (traditional rural landscape) area — free to enter even without a Ghibli Park
  ticket. Given the day is fully dedicated to Ghibli Park, this is more of an
  ambient note than a separate stop, but worth knowing the surrounding grounds
  are green rather than a standalone parking-lot theme park.

# Nagoya City

## Nagoya City — reconvene-day free time

Actually usable: on the reconvene day (arriving from Osaka, before friend's flight
lands from Hokkaido), the Osaka→Nagoya shinkansen is quick, so there's likely a real
window of free time depending on her arrival time. Best pick for this slot:
**Atsuta Shrine** (see below) — hits both traditional-sites and nature, and is easy
to cut short or leave whenever her flight status firms up, unlike something with a
fixed showtime.

## Recommendations by theme

### Food (vegetarian-relevant heads-up)
- Nagoya's signature dishes — **miso katsu**, **tebasaki** (fried chicken wings),
  **hitsumabushi** (grilled eel) — are all meat/fish, not vegetarian-friendly by
  default. Don't expect to sample "Nagoya meshi" as-is.
- **Osu Shopping Street** — the city's most international food neighborhood, home
  to halal grocers and a growing cluster of Indian, Nepali, and Middle Eastern
  kitchens; the most reliable area for vegetarian options specifically, rather than
  adapting traditional Nagoya dishes.
- A few dedicated vegetarian/vegan spots exist scattered around the city — worth
  pinning specific ones once an actual free-time slot is confirmed.

### Traditional / historic
- **Nagoya Castle** — one of Japan's major castles, though largely a reconstruction
  (the original keep was lost in WWII); still a notable landmark if a castle visit
  fits (recall Matsuyama Castle is already "if convenient" elsewhere on the trip,
  so this would be a second castle option, not necessarily both).
- **Atsuta Shrine** — one of Shinto's most important shrines, said to house one of
  Japan's three sacred imperial treasures; quieter and less touristed than the
  Kyoto/Nara shrines already on the itinerary.

### Nature (group loves this category)
- **Atsuta Shrine grounds** — heavily forested with old-growth trees, similar in
  spirit to Kasugayama Forest in Nara; a genuine green space attached to the shrine
  visit above.
- **Nagoya Castle grounds** — moat and garden areas around the castle, similar to
  the Osaka Castle Park note elsewhere.

### Pop culture / design (fits stated interests)
- **Osu** doubles as a retro/subculture shopping district (vintage, anime,
  electronics) alongside its food scene — some overlap with the
  Akihabara/Nakano-Broadway pull already noted for Tokyo, if more of that flavor is
  wanted in a second city.
